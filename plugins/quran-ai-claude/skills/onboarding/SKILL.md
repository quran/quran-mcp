# Begin studying with quran.ai

Make the first encounter a conversation with the interactive book: **Read,
Select, Ask, Discover**, then return to the text through links in the answer.
Follow the user's immediate purpose; a study starter calls for a meaningful
reader-and-chat experience. A settings question calls for the relevant help.

## Three ways to begin

Recognize these requests, shorter starter wording, and natural paraphrases by
meaning. A pasted exact phrase is not required. Use additional context in the
user's request to shape the experience.

| Starter | Approach |
|---|---|
| Guide my Qur'an discovery journey with quran.ai's reader and study tools, drawing on our past conversations to suggest some initial topics and passages I might be interested in exploring. | A personal discovery journey. |
| Take me to an unexpected, powerful Quran verse; help me linger, reflect, and notice more. | A fresh passage for reflection. |
| What does the Quran claim about God? Help me explore its claims with an open mind, and let my honest follow-up questions guide where we go next. | An open inquiry about God. |

**A personal discovery journey.** Use relevant interests, questions, language
preferences, and circumstances from the current conversation or available
memory of past conversations. In the very first response, open a fitting
passage and immediately suggest several distinct topics and passages the user
might be interested in exploring. Explain each possibility substantively, with
roughly three to five verified reader links embedded in the prose, spanning
meaningful ayahs, passages, or surah openings. These are possible starting
points, not a fixed reading path or settled claims about the user's interests.
Mention a connection to shared context when it helps, without reciting private
background or inferring their religion or beliefs. If relevant history is
unavailable, offer useful starting options without pretending to personalize
them. Use the reader and appropriate study tools together; do not defer the
topic suggestions to another turn or replace them with an intake interview
or a tool tutorial. Leave the user free to choose or redirect the journey.

**A fresh passage for reflection.** Choose an unexpected, powerful verse or
short passage that rewards close reading. Vary choices rather than always
reaching for the same familiar verse; do not assume it must match the user's
current concerns. Fetch it and any surrounding passage needed to understand
it. Invite an unhurried second reading: help the user linger over an image,
phrase, contrast, or question and notice something they might have missed.
Guide reflection in ordinary language, keeping personal reflection distinct
from the text and attributed interpretation. Keep the opening focused, with
the passage link and one or two useful linked connections. Treat a surprising
choice as an invitation to reflect, not a personal divine message or prediction.

**An open inquiry about God.** Begin with what the Quran claims about God,
using a short, grounded opening passage and linked passages that illuminate
those claims. Address any specific question the user has already expressed.
Help them explore and understand with an open mind, without assuming belief
or agreement. Present the claims and supporting reasoning clearly, take doubts
seriously, and distinguish the text from commentary and your own reflection.
Welcome honest follow-up questions and let them guide what to examine next;
explore revelation, Jesus, or spiritual purpose when relevant to those questions.
Leave room to consider and challenge what they read rather than directing them
through a predetermined tour or scripted debate.

## Deliver the first study experience

Read the [Interactive Study skill](../interactive-study/SKILL.md) and follow its
**Read, select, ask, discover** workflow for all three starters. It owns reader
opening, the continuing conversation, selection context, grounding, and exact
deep-link handling. Complete both the reader opening and the thoughtful
response; the starter has not been answered by rendering the reader alone.

Use [Tafsir source guide](../grounding-rules/SKILL.md#tafsir-source-guide)
to select commentary for the user's actual inquiry. Close reading may need linguistic,
rhetorical, or structural analysis; questions about God may need theological arguments
and differing interpretations; practical or reflective questions may call for guidance
on conduct, worship, or inner life. Let follow-up questions change the source selection.

Keep the introduction approachable, often about 250–400 words unless the
user's request calls for something else. Weave the reader links into the
explanation at the points where the user should look, compare, or explore.
Include one concrete invitation to select a particular phrase in the opening
passage and ask a meaningful question about it, followed by freedom to choose
another phrase or direction. Offer two or three tailored next questions,
including a way to go deeper and a way to steer elsewhere. The follow-up
returns to the same study loop rather than restarting onboarding.

## Setup and later help

Defaults are ready to use. Leave saved preferences unchanged unless the user
asks to change them; skipping setup never blocks study. New readers follow
System appearance (Navy or Ivory), dock contextual meaning at the top in Word
meanings mode, and start with Study tools open in Quick send mode. The reader
guide opens only when requested.
An empty meaning lane is initially collapsed; Arabic language or region hints
can also make Collapse lane default to on. Existing saved choices take precedence.

For **Set up** without a more specific goal, give a brief introduction through
the personal starting-point flow; no intake questionnaire is required. If the
user wants only an opening or navigation, follow the Interactive Study skill's
quiet-opening rule. Do not add a guided session to an explicit skip or a
controls-only request.

For settings, navigation, selection, attachments, or other reader-control
questions, read [Reader controls and settings](references/reader-help.md) and
answer only the relevant part. Do not open another reader merely to explain
its controls. Mention that they can ask “help with the reader” when useful.
The host's Set up action invokes this skill; it is not a permanent help button.
